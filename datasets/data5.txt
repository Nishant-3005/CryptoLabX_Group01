In the shadow of silicon valleys and data centers that hum like living things, two analysts
sat hunched over glowing terminals. The encrypted intercept had arrived at 03:17 hours, tagged
PRIORITY ALPHA. Neither of them had slept.

"The key length is six," said Meera, pushing her glasses up. "Index of Coincidence confirms it."
Rajan nodded, fingers already dancing across the keyboard. "Then we run the Kasiski test to
verify. If it checks out, we split the ciphertext into six streams and attack each with standard
frequency analysis."

The room smelled of cold coffee and dry-erase markers. Outside, the city slept, unaware that
the message sitting in their queue could change the course of three ongoing investigations.

"E, T, A," Meera muttered, eyes fixed on the frequency histogram on her screen. "The most
common ciphertext letter in stream one is X. If X maps to E, our shift for that stream is
twenty-three." She paused. "Or negative three. Same thing mod twenty-six."

Rajan smiled. This was the part he loved. Not the math — the math was easy once you knew it.
It was the moment of revelation. When gibberish became language. When noise became signal.
When a message written for no eyes but one pair became readable to anyone with the right key.

"Got it," he said quietly. The plaintext bloomed across his screen, word by careful word.
"It reads: The package moves at midnight. Dock seven. Come alone."

They looked at each other. The work was only beginning.
